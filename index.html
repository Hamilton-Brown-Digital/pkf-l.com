The start-up phase

As a start-up you will need an easy-to-use system which delivers reliability and compliance, and gives you the time to concentrate on running your business.

Cloud based accounting tools such as Xero, QuickBooks and Sage provide off the shelf packages to meet basic compliance requirements. Whilst providing visibility over the financial position without requiring vast accounting knowledge or financial expertise.

Before adopting any software, you should plan ahead so you don’t outgrow your system too soon. Considering some of the following will help ensure you are proactive in your choices:

  • Scalability

    Choose software that allows growth in users, transaction volumes and add-ons without the need for a full migration.

  • Automation and integrations

    Bank feeds, invoicing, VAT submissions, payroll and payment gateways should connect seamlessly and reduce manual work.

  • Smarter insights

    Look for built-in analytics/AI to help auto-categorise transactions, flag anomalies, and highlight trends, allowing your bookkeeping data to help you make quick decisions.

Outcome: Simple, compliant finances with time back for building the business.

The right time to scale

As your start-up grows, simple accounting systems will eventually reach its limits. Changes in finance, operations, reporting and processes may indicate that it is time to upgrade to a more advanced system. Successful companies often reach a tipping point where manual workarounds, slow reporting, and spreadsheet sprawl signal it’s time for change.

The move to a mid-market system isn’t just a software upgrade, it’s a strategic step towards efficiency, insight, and sustainable growth with mid-market solutions helping to deliver deeper financial capability without the cost of an ERP system.

The following are common trigger points that signal when a business might consider moving beyond basic tools to support growth, efficiency and additional compliance that comes with growth:

  • Multi-entity complexity

    Managing multiple entities or regions incorporated into manual consolidations slowing month/year-end.

  • Granular reporting

    Increased requirement of visibility by project, department or product line with this information shoehorned from data silos

  • Workflow automation

    Approvals, recurring journals and close tasks need to run reliably with a clear audit trail.

  • International operations

    Multi-currency, tax, and multi-GAAP requirements

  • Integrations

    Finance needs real-time connections to customer relationship management (CRM), payroll, banking and operations via robust APIs.

  • Controls and governance

    Investors or boards expect stronger permissions, segregation of duties, regulatory compliance, controls and audit readiness.

Outgrowing a finance system is a positive step and reflects growth and change in the business. Recognising the signs early allows you to act strategically, not reactively. A mid-market platform delivers faster month-end close, deeper insights, reduced manual workload, and scalable operation, freeing your finance team to focus on what matters most, and supporting your growth further.

Enterprise Resource Planning

At a glance

  • End-to-end
  • Cross-department integration Finance, inventory, supply chain, HR, customer relationship management
  • Advanced forecasting
  • Auditability at scale

When finance must orchestrate the whole business

ERP stands for Enterprise Resource Planning and is an integrated software system designed to manage and streamline all core business processes across various departments.

Benefits:

  • Instead of using separate tools for accounting, inventory, supply chain, HR, and CRM, an ERP consolidates these functions into a single unified platform within one ecosystem.

  • ERP systems are highly customisable and can be tailored to fit your organisation’s precise needs.

Challenges to consider:

  • Complex implementation

    Requires significant time and resources for setup and training. Implementation timelines need to be robust and supported to avoid delays in any effective go live date.

  • Change management

    Employees may resist new processes; proper training and communication are essential to ensure buy-in and engagement.

  • Customisation needs

    To get the most benefit to your business, additional development to customise/integrate within your existing systems, reporting or operational needs will be required.

  • Ongoing maintenance

    Regular updates and support are necessary to keep the system running smoothly.

  • Costs

    ERP systems can be expensive to purchase, implement and further ongoing costs for licensing/subscriptions can be high.

Outcome: Simple, compliant finances with time back for building the business.

Decision roadmap

Step-by-step guide when considering implementing a new system

01 Assess current pain points

Review your existing processes: Identify inefficiencies, data silos, compliance risks, and manual workload. Engage with departmental staff and gather well rounded feedback from finance, operations, HR and IT teams.

02 Define key priorities

Clarify what you want to achieve from any switch of accounting system: automation, faster reporting, better forecasting, improved controls and compliance, scalability.

03 Evaluate current systems

Determine if existing processes and systems can be optimised first or if additional integration within these systems would be advantageous and possible before changing systems.

04 Cost-benefit analysis

Compare the cost of the new system, including implementation costs and time required, against potential savings and productivity gains.

05 Research solutions fully

Shortlist vendors based on your business needs, capabilities, industry fit, scalability, cost and functionality.

06 Engage stakeholders early

It is important to involve key personnel across finance, operations, IT, and leadership early to ensure alignment and buy-in from all parties.

07 Change management

Develop a training and communication strategy to ease adoption and ensure minimal resistance to change from existing engagement teams.

08 Implementation roadmap

Define clear timelines, allocated resources, and staged milestones for deployment.

09 Pilot and rollout

Test the system in each department or location to identify issues before scaling and fully deploying to go live with the new system.

10 Monitor and optimise

Continuously review performance, obtain user feedback, and assess Return On Investment post-implementation.

Accounting systems are not a one-size-fits-all. They should evolve alongside your business, supporting growth without creating unnecessary complexity.

For start-ups, simplicity and cost-effectiveness are key; for SMEs, scalability and integration become priorities; and for larger organisations, ERP systems deliver the visibility and control needed to manage enterprise wide operations.

Implementing any accounting system is a strategic decision and one that requires careful planning, stakeholder engagement, and a clear understanding of both benefits and challenges. By following a structured evaluation process and aligning technology with business objectives, companies can transform their financial management into a powerful driver of efficiency, compliance, and long-term success.
